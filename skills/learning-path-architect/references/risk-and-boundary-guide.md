# Risk and Boundary Guide

Use this reference when a learning path involves medicine, law, finance, aviation, cybersecurity operations, industrial systems, hazardous equipment, regulated practice, personal data, or other high-consequence work.

## Planning rules

| Risk condition | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| The learner may act on people, money, legal rights, safety systems, or sensitive data | Separate educational objectives from authorization to practice. Identify supervision, licensing, institutional policy, or access controls that lie outside the syllabus. |
| The domain changes through regulations, standards, APIs, or live threats | Check current authoritative sources before presenting details as requirements and record the freshness boundary. |
| Practice could cause harm if performed incorrectly | Use simulations, sandboxes, case analysis, or supervised practice before real-world execution. Mark prohibited or unsafe shortcuts explicitly. |
| The learner requests professional authority or certification | Define the knowledge and performance evidence the map can develop, but do not promise licensure, certification, employment, or professional competence from syllabus completion alone. |
| The request involves offensive security, evasion, or dual-use capabilities | Keep objectives bounded to authorized, defensive, educational, or sandboxed contexts and avoid operational details that enable harm. |

## Output safeguards

State the relevant limitation briefly in assumptions or coverage notes. Prefer safe practice environments and observable, reversible exercises. Do not turn a warning into a generic disclaimer on every map; activate this guide only when the domain or requested action makes it relevant.

## Evidence boundaries

Use stronger evidence gates for higher-consequence domains. A completed tutorial, self-test, or simulated artifact may demonstrate learning progress but does not establish authorization, legal compliance, clinical judgment, operational readiness, or professional accountability. Identify those boundaries directly and proportionately.
