# Planning policy layer

Use this reference when the learner wants to control how routes are generated without changing the authoritative portfolio.

A policy may set progression posture (conservative, balanced, or aggressive), concurrency mode (single-track, bounded parallel, or exploratory), route style (theory-first, project-first, or mixed), objective emphasis (credential, usable capability, research, breadth, or exploration), maintenance tolerance, maximum weekly context switches, acceptable uncertainty, response detail, and default visible scope.

Policies are preferences and constraints, not facts. Store their source, effective date, scope, and override conditions. If a policy conflicts with a safety boundary, dependency, evidence requirement, or explicit goal, preserve the goal and explain the trade-off rather than silently violating the boundary. Scenario comparison may test a new policy before it becomes authoritative.
