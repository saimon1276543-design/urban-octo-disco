# Learning Science for Roadmap Design

Use this reference only for roadmap or study-plan requests. It governs how the learner reinforces and transfers a capability; it does not replace dependency analysis, proficiency evidence, or the freshness protocol.

## Retrieval before review

Turn review into **retrieval practice** whenever the goal is durable recall or fluent performance. Ask the learner to reconstruct an explanation, derive a procedure, debug a novel example, or produce an artifact without first rereading the answer. Re-reading may be used afterward to correct errors, but it is not sufficient evidence of retention.

## Spacing after demonstrated readiness

After a milestone’s exit criterion is met, add a revisit loop when retention matters: retrieve or re-solve the capability later, then apply it in a related context. Do not fabricate calendar schedules when the user has not supplied a time horizon. Use relative triggers such as **after the next milestone**, **at the next practice session**, or **after a meaningful interval**. If the user provides a schedule, concrete intervals such as 7 and 30 days may be used as a plan rather than as a universal rule.

Do not use spacing to advance past an unmet prerequisite. First remediate the smallest blocking gap; then schedule reinforcement after the criterion is met.

## Interleaving and transfer

Once a learner can perform a skill in a familiar context, interleave it with neighboring problem types or alternate implementations. The purpose is discrimination and transfer, not arbitrary variety. Keep early Novice work sufficiently bounded that interleaving does not obscure the core pattern.

## Deliberate practice and difficulty calibration

Choose practice that is just beyond fluent independent performance but still diagnosable. Increase ambiguity, novelty, constraints, or judgment only after the learner can meet the current exit criterion. For lower levels, reduce the support gradually; for higher levels, remove worked examples and require trade-offs, debugging, evaluation, and explanation of decisions.

A roadmap should state the support transition when it materially affects the target level:

| Stage | Typical support | Evidence focus |
|---|---|---|
| Guided | Worked example, checklist, or close feedback | Accurate execution and explanation |
| Familiar independent | Reference material allowed, known problem pattern | Reliable completion without step-by-step prompting |
| Bounded unfamiliar | Limited support, new but comparable task | Transfer, debugging, and adaptation |
| Ambiguous or open-ended | Constraints and evaluation criteria, not a recipe | Trade-offs, judgment, robustness, and communication |

## Roadmap insertion rule

Add a short **Retention and transfer loop** after major milestones only when the user asks how to study, when the goal is long-term retention, or when the path is long enough that forgetting is a material risk. Keep it concise: retrieval action, trigger for revisiting, and the next transfer context. Do not turn every leaf into flashcards or impose a universal schedule.

## Boundary

Learning-science guidance supports practice design; it does not establish that a learner is competent, certified, licensed, or safe to perform high-consequence work. Use the risk-and-boundary guide for those decisions.
