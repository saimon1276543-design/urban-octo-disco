# BCI and Neurology Foundations

Use this reference for brain-computer interfaces, neuroscience, neurology, neurotechnology, neural signals, and neurorehabilitation learning paths. Preserve the distinction between basic science, engineering research, clinical education, and regulated clinical practice.

## BCI domain architecture

A BCI route should connect, but not collapse, the following layers:

1. **Neuroscience foundation:** neuroanatomy, neurophysiology, cellular and molecular neuroscience, synapses, sensory and motor systems, autonomic systems, neuroplasticity, learning, sleep, and cognition.
2. **Neurological function:** localization, networks, motor control, sensation, language, memory, consciousness, behavior, and interactions between brain, spinal cord, peripheral nerves, and muscle.
3. **Signal and measurement:** EEG, ECoG, intracortical signals, local field potentials, spike activity, EMG, eye and motion signals, fNIRS, MEG, and multimodal acquisition. Cover sampling, noise, artifacts, impedance, reference choices, calibration, synchronization, and measurement limits.
4. **BCI engineering:** electrodes and materials, analog front ends, amplifiers, digitization, filtering, feature extraction, decoding, classifiers, adaptive control, feedback, human-computer interaction, embedded systems, robotics, and real-time software.
5. **Human and clinical translation:** usability, accessibility, patient and caregiver needs, neurorehabilitation, assistive communication, informed consent, human factors, clinical endpoints, safety, reproducibility, and regulatory/ethical review.

## Neurology foundations

A durable neurology curriculum should include neuroanatomy and localization; neurophysiology; neurologic examination concepts; motor, sensory, cranial-nerve, coordination, gait, language, cognition, and autonomic systems; seizure and epilepsy concepts; stroke and cerebrovascular disease; headache; movement disorders; demyelinating and inflammatory disease; neurodegeneration; neuromuscular and peripheral-nerve disease; spinal-cord disorders; infection; tumors; trauma; sleep; neurogenetics; pediatric and aging neurology; neurocritical care; rehabilitation; and diagnostic reasoning.

Teach symptom-to-localization reasoning, differential diagnosis, test selection, uncertainty, red flags, and communication as distinct capabilities. Clinical examination, diagnosis, treatment, and patient management require supervised professional education and authorization; a self-study roadmap or BCI project cannot substitute for those requirements.

## Signal-processing and ML bridge

Connect neural signals to sampling theory, time/frequency analysis, filtering, artifact rejection, feature extraction, dimensionality reduction, classification/regression, calibration, cross-validation, subject transfer, distribution shift, uncertainty, adaptive decoders, and online evaluation. Prevent leakage across trials, sessions, and subjects. Report missing data, artifact policy, preprocessing latency, calibration burden, confidence, and failure cases.

A decoder's offline score does not establish real-world usability, clinical benefit, safety, or generalization. Require held-out participants or sessions, temporal validation, baseline comparisons, error analysis, robustness checks, and human-factors evidence appropriate to the claim.

## Neurotechnology and materials bridge

Connect BCI hardware to electrochemistry and metallic materials through electrode/electrolyte/tissue interfaces, charge transfer, polarization, impedance, corrosion, coatings, surface modification, mechanical mismatch, encapsulation, biocompatibility, heating, stimulation limits, failure analysis, and measurement artifacts. Keep implant safety, sterilization, long-term reliability, and clinical translation as separate evidence tracks.

## Learning outcomes and evidence

Examples of bounded evidence include a labeled neural-signal dataset with provenance; a reproducible preprocessing and artifact-reporting pipeline; a simulator for a decoder; a cross-session evaluation; a human-in-the-loop usability study under approved protocol; a literature synthesis with quality appraisal; or a materials-interface experiment using safe, approved procedures. Each artifact must state what it demonstrates and what it does not demonstrate.

## Safety, ethics, and current overlays

Require approved or synthetic data where appropriate, privacy and de-identification, consent, human-subjects review, secure storage, device and electrical safety, non-maleficence, accessibility, fairness, data ownership, and transparent uncertainty. Current diagnostic criteria, treatment guidelines, device regulations, institutional curricula, and clinical trial requirements are volatile overlays requiring authoritative, dated sources.
