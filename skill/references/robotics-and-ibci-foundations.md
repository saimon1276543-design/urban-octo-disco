# Robotics and iBCI Foundations

Use this reference for integrated robotics/iBCI routes while preserving separate domain cores.

## Robotics core

Mathematics, mechanics, electronics, embedded systems, sensing, calibration, state estimation, Kalman filtering, kinematics, dynamics, control, planning, perception, manipulation, mobile robots, simulation, integration, testing, and safety. Catmull–Rom splines and similar interpolation belong where trajectory or graphics outcomes justify them.

## iBCI core

Neuroanatomy, neurophysiology, neural signals, acquisition, analog front ends, filtering, artifacts, signal processing, decoding, ML, embedded systems, human factors, ethics, clinical translation, and reproducible validation. For the broader neurology and BCI route, load `references/bci-and-neurology-foundations.md`, which adds neurological localization, examination concepts, disease families, clinical reasoning, neurorehabilitation, measurement modalities, human factors, and translation boundaries.

## BCI and neurology bridge

Keep basic neuroscience, neurological function, signal measurement, BCI engineering, and clinical translation as linked but independently evidenced layers. A neural decoder, EEG pipeline, electrode experiment, simulator, or assistive interface can demonstrate a technical capability without demonstrating diagnosis, treatment competence, implant safety, clinical benefit, or permission to practice.

Require held-out session/participant evaluation, leakage prevention, artifact and preprocessing disclosure, calibration and latency reporting, uncertainty, failure analysis, usability evidence, privacy, consent, and appropriate supervision when a route involves human data or participants.

## Bridge and boundary

Electrochemistry/materials covers electrode/electrolyte/tissue interfaces, charge transfer, polarization, impedance, corrosion, metallic materials, coatings, surface science, biocompatibility, and failure. A biosignal dashboard, decoder, robot simulator, or materials experiment is a bounded integration artifact; it does not prove clinical competence, implant safety, or authorization.

Use synthetic, simulated, or approved data where appropriate and preserve supervision, privacy, human-subjects, and device-safety boundaries.
