# Proficiency Levels

Use one target level for the syllabus unless the user explicitly requests a progression across multiple levels. The labels describe the expected end capability, not the learner’s current self-description. They are practical planning bands, not universal certification standards; calibrate them to the domain, task, and evidence context rather than treating them as interchangeable with a formal credential.

| Level | Equivalent | End-state expectation | Syllabus emphasis | Evidence standard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Novice** | Beginner | Understands essential vocabulary and mental models, follows guided procedures, and completes narrowly scoped tasks with support. | Orientation, core concepts, setup, worked examples, safety, and tightly scaffolded practice. Keep prerequisites minimal and avoid assuming fluency. | Explain basic ideas, follow a procedure, and complete a small guided task with limited correction. |
| **Advanced Beginner** | Lower Intermediate | Handles familiar tasks independently, adapts examples to nearby situations, and diagnoses common errors with reference material. | Foundational fluency, recurring workflows, common variations, troubleshooting patterns, and small integrated exercises. | Complete a familiar task independently and adapt it to one comparable variation. |
| **Competent** | Medium/Solid Intermediate | Performs routine work reliably, chooses among common approaches, and solves moderately unfamiliar problems. | Integrated concepts, trade-offs, testing, debugging, independent projects, and realistic constraints. | Produce a working solution to a new but bounded problem and justify key decisions. |
| **Proficient** | Upper Intermediate | Handles complex or ambiguous work, selects appropriate methods, optimizes quality, and explains trade-offs to others. | Advanced patterns, architecture, edge cases, performance, maintainability, cross-domain integration, and review practice. | Deliver a robust solution under realistic constraints, defend decisions, and diagnose non-obvious failures. |
| **Expert** | Advanced | Creates or evaluates approaches in novel situations, works at system or field level, and contributes original judgment or knowledge. | Deep theory, state-of-the-art or specialist practice, research gaps, system design, adversarial cases, mentoring, and original work. | Solve open-ended problems, compare competing approaches, establish evaluation criteria, and produce defensible original or authoritative work. |

## Application rules

First identify the requested target level and preserve the user’s wording. If no level is given, infer the lowest level consistent with the requested outcome and state the assumption. Do not equate topic count with proficiency. Increase level through changes in independence, ambiguity, transfer, scope, judgment, and evidence—not by merely adding more subtopics.

A learner may be advanced in one branch and novice in another. Apply **Skip if demonstrated** or branch-specific diagnostics rather than assigning one global level when the evidence is mixed. A syllabus may use staged targets, such as Novice foundations leading to Competent application, but must label the transition, performance context, and exit evidence. Do not infer a level from familiarity, topic completion, or a single polished artifact alone.

For Expert requests, do not promise professional authority, licensure, or real-world expertise solely from a syllabus. Define the knowledge and performance evidence the map can develop, and identify experience, mentorship, or domain exposure that lies outside the map.
