# Learner-facing explanation layer

Translate internal graph and runtime decisions into plain language. For each consequential choice answer, when relevant: why this is here; why it comes before another item; what can be skipped or postponed; what proves readiness; what changed; what happens if the learner pauses; what the current action is; and what uncertainty remains.

Use technical IDs and metrics in the record, but lead the learner-facing response with the practical consequence. Replace terms such as bottleneck centrality, stale dependency, and uncertainty propagation with explanations such as “many later skills depend on this,” “this tool detail may have changed,” and “because this assumption is uncertain, the plan is using a pilot instead of a permanent commitment.”
