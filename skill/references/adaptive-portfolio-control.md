# Evidence-driven adaptive portfolio control

Use this reference when a learner wants the plan to learn from performance, compare routes experimentally, compress a huge portfolio into usable views, or decide whether a capability is truly ready to unlock downstream work.

## Capability states

Record the strongest state supported by evidence, not by topic completion:

| State | Meaning | Typical transition evidence |
|---|---|---|
| unfamiliar | No reliable exposure or baseline | proportional diagnostic |
| exposed | Can recognize or describe the idea | retrieval or explanation |
| guided | Can perform with examples, checklist, or close support | guided task |
| practiced | Can perform repeated bounded practice | independent familiar task |
| transferable | Can apply to a new comparable context | bounded transfer |
| retained | Can retrieve and use after spacing or interruption | delayed recheck |
| maintained | Remains usable through planned retrieval or reuse | maintenance sample |
| stale | Previously evidenced but not rechecked within the relevant decay window | revalidation task |
| contradicted | Later evidence conflicts with the prior state | diagnostic or remediation |

A capability may carry more than one label in history, but its current state must include context, support level, evidence date, confidence, and the smallest unresolved gap. Do not unlock a downstream node merely because an earlier node is marked completed.

## Experiment-driven route selection

When two routes are plausible or the portfolio is overloaded, propose a bounded pilot rather than making a large irreversible change. A pilot contains a hypothesis, one route change, duration or cycle boundary, capacity cap, success evidence, failure signal, stop rule, and next decision. Change one major dimension at a time: branch count, route order, session pattern, tool choice, or project scope. Preserve the unselected route and its reversal condition.

After the pilot, compare planned versus observed effort, evidence quality, transfer, retention, switching cost, blocker frequency, learner-reported usability, and route leverage. Choose continue, revise, stop, or revert. A pilot is evidence about the route under the tested context, not proof of universal mastery or permanent capacity.

## Personal capacity learning

Maintain a capacity profile with nominal availability, reliable availability, usable learning capacity, cognitive load tolerance, recovery reserve, setup overhead, interruption rate, context-switch cost, and sustainable branch count. For each estimate record whether it is stated, observed, inferred, or unknown. Update one parameter at a time when possible. Use robust multi-cycle patterns; do not let one unusually good or bad cycle rewrite the whole plan.

If speed improves without retention or transfer, do not automatically add branches. If capacity falls, protect the north star and completed evidence, reduce concurrency, and move optional work to maintenance or deferred states. Treat unexplained variance as uncertainty until observations identify a smaller failing assumption.

## Synchronized portfolio views

Maintain one authoritative index and derive three learner-facing views from stable IDs:

1. **Portfolio view:** all goals, domains, route states, dependencies, evidence states, and deferred scope.
2. **Phase view:** the current phase, active branches, relevant prerequisites, maintenance obligations, and phase exit evidence.
3. **Today view:** one primary action, at most one compatible parallel action, stopping boundary, evidence target, blocker response, and review trigger.

Compress what is displayed, not what is authoritative. Every compressed item must remain addressable by stable ID. When a view is stale, show its revision and regenerate only the affected slice.

## Unlock and maintenance rules

Unlock only the next capability supported by the required evidence context. Keep evidence that is weaker than the target as a diagnostic or partial state rather than deleting it. Completed capabilities may move to maintained or stale; maintenance is part of usable capacity and is not a second full syllabus. Recompute the active frontier after material evidence, capacity, route, or freshness changes.

## Safety and scope

Experiments must be low-risk, reversible, and appropriate to the domain. In clinical, human-subject, security, hardware, or regulated contexts, use simulation, synthetic data, supervision, authorization, and bounded environments. A pilot can test a learning route; it cannot grant licensure, professional authority, or permission to perform consequential work.
