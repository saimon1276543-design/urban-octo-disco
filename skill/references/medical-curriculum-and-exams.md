# Medical Curriculum and Exams

Use this reference for AIIMS-style MBBS learning architecture, INI-CET, and NEET-PG preparation.

## Durable curriculum architecture

Organize foundational biomedical sciences, organ systems, pathology, pharmacology, microbiology, forensic and community medicine, clinical medicine, surgery, allied specialties, diagnostics, therapeutics, communication, professionalism, public health, evidence-based medicine, and integrated cases. Add clinical skills and supervised practice as distinct evidence, not as reading topics.

Neurology should be represented as an explicit clinical-science branch spanning neuroanatomy, neurophysiology, localization, neurologic examination concepts, motor and sensory systems, cranial nerves, cognition, language, gait, autonomic function, epilepsy, stroke, headache, movement disorders, demyelinating and inflammatory disease, neurodegeneration, neuromuscular and peripheral-nerve disease, spinal-cord disorders, infection, tumors, trauma, sleep, neurogenetics, pediatric and aging neurology, neurocritical care, rehabilitation, and diagnostic reasoning. Connect this branch to `references/bci-and-neurology-foundations.md` when the learner also requests BCI, neurotechnology, neural signals, or neurorehabilitation.

## Exam routes

Share the medical foundation but keep exam-specific retrieval, image interpretation, clinical vignettes, question-bank analysis, error logs, mixed practice, revision cycles, and mocks distinct. Current eligibility, dates, formats, subject distributions, counselling, and official notices are volatile and require current authoritative sources.

## Boundary

An AIIMS Delhi-aligned architecture is an educational target model, not a claim about an official current syllabus. A generated plan does not confer medical qualification, rank, licensure, clinical authority, or permission to practice.

Neurology learning plans must distinguish basic science, case-based reasoning, supervised clinical examination, diagnosis, treatment, research, and device engineering. Self-study, a BCI project, a signal decoder, or a literature review cannot substitute for supervised clinical education, institutional review, licensure, or patient-care authorization.
